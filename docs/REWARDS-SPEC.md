# Rewards, keys and XP

The design behind two features in `BUILD-PLAN.md`: **Rewards: the keys pass**
(R2) and **Rewards at runtime** (R3).

> **ALL THREE PHASES ARE BUILT.** This document is now the design record rather
> than a plan. What shipped differs from what is written below in four places,
> all of them recorded against the two `BUILD-PLAN.md` features:
>
> - **No goal slice** (§6). The pot is `placed + allowance` exactly, with no
>   third term. `goalItemValue` prices the objective above the largest single
>   reward in the dungeon instead, so it cannot be out-earned by a strongbox in
>   a side room whatever the pot rolled.
> - **A reward can be carried by a creature** (§4). `Reward.holder` is a room or
>   a mobile.
> - **Reachability is blocked ways only** (§4.1), exactly as written there.
> - **The goal-location key was `roomIds` all along** (§7.3). A region challenge
>   carries the rooms its chart covers, because by the time one is drawn nothing
>   is left holding the chunk map.
>
> The one thing §10 left as a dial and the build did not settle is the
> **placed-key spread**: 1 to 9 at size 5, not the "roughly four" §5 predicts.
> The cause is the §4 order working as specified. See the keys-pass feature.

Three things are being built at once because they are one idea seen from three
sides: **what a dungeon gives you**, **what a dungeon asks of you**, and **what
it pays for answering**. The current reward system knows only the first, and it
distributes it by a taxonomy with no relationship to anything else in the
dungeon.

---

## 1. What is wrong now

`distributeRewards` (`src/generation/content/stock.ts:830`) spends a conserved
budget `K = max(3, round(0.55 × rooms))` into five parcel types — material,
knowledge, leverage, boon, relic — each carrying a `ParcelGrade` that is the
sole input to what the thing is worth (`rollItemValue` → `VALUE_BANDS`,
`src/rules/knave/equipment.ts:1082`).

Two problems.

**Nothing points at anything.** A dungeon's rewards and a dungeon's problems are
generated by different code that never meets. The single exception is the
`knowledge` parcel that rolls "Enemy weakness" and sets `aboutMobileId` to a real
roster creature — one hand-wired instance of the mechanism this phase
generalises.

**Money is unbounded.** Grade alone sets value, so a dungeon's total take is
whatever its grades happened to roll: three significant parcels is 3,600–36,000cp
against a Knave starting purse of 60–360.

---

## 2. Challenges

A **challenge** is one keyable property of one entity — not the entity. A
creature with a rolled weakness and a rolled ability is two challenges; a
creature with two weaknesses is two more.

| challenge | source | keyable property |
|---|---|---|
| creature weakness | `MonsterSeed.weaknesses` | each entry |
| creature ability | `MonsterSeed.traitsAndAbilities` (from `MONSTER_ABILITIES`) | each entry |
| NPC liability | `NpcSeed.liability` | the entry |
| NPC asset | `NpcSeed.asset` | the entry |
| blocked way | `StockedConnector.gated` | the blocker |
| concealed way | `StockedConnector.hidden` | the concealment |
| hazard | `SpecialContent.kind === "hazard"` | the hazard, or the trap's trigger |
| obstacle | `SpecialContent.kind === "obstacle"` | the puzzle |
| region | chunk | the layout |
| goal location | the goal room | where it is |
| goal properties | the goal entity | **not enumerable at R1 — see §6** |

`gateConnectors` gates `E/6`–`E/4` of the edges, so a size 5 dungeon (30 rooms,
~40 edges) enumerates roughly 30 challenges: ~8 blocked ways, ~5 hazards, ~4
obstacles, ~6 creature properties, ~2 concealed ways, 5 regions, plus the goal's.

The enumeration is the phase's real product. Everything else reads from it: keys
answer challenges, accomplishments resolve them, XP prices them, and generation
can assert that no challenge is unanswerable and no key sits behind the thing it
opens.

### 2.1 Obstacles get signposted

An obstacle is currently an unmarked `IRoomFeature` — only the generation-time
`ElementBinding` knows it is a puzzle, and bindings do not survive into play. It
gets an explicit marker on the entity and a `resolved` field, whitelisted in
`src/gameplay/diffs/paths.ts`.

## 3. Reward taxonomy

Two families. `ParcelType` and `ParcelGrade` are both cut.

```
KEY       answers one challenge
GENERAL   50/50 equipment / treasure
```

`leverage` and `boon` do not survive. Leverage ("defensible chokepoint", "safe
place to rest") describes a room, and the content pass can write that without
spending a reward slot. Boons are consumables — potions, and nothing else — and
they are drawn as **treasure**, not as equipment. See 3.1.

### 3.1 Equipment

**As written this was six rows; it is five.** The potion row was cut and moved to
3.2, because equipment is priced from `STANDARD_EQUIPMENT` and there is no
catalog entry for "Potion of Ghost-speech" — the row was pricing itself out of
`KEY_VALUE_BAND` (5–60cp), which is what a key is worth and not what a flask of
flight is worth. Drawn as treasure it takes a share of the conserved pot like
everything else the dungeon puts a number on. With it went `EquipmentDraw`'s
third variant, `rolled`, which nothing else produced.

Five rows, all from tables already in the repo:

1. a weapon — `WEAPONS` / `WEAPON_KEYS` (`tables.ts`)
2. armor, helmet, or shield — `rollArmorTable` / `rollHelmetShieldTable`
3. a spellbook — `SPELLS`, the d100 table
4. 1–3 dungeoneering gear — `DUNGEONEERING_GEAR`
5. 2–4 general gear — `GENERAL_GEAR_1` / `GENERAL_GEAR_2`

Equipment takes its price from `STANDARD_EQUIPMENT`, always, and does not touch
the treasure pot.

### 3.2 Treasure

```
1. coins
2. treasure item
3. treasure item + trait / valuable material
4. magical ingredients
5. a potion — POTIONS
```

Rows 4 and 5 are the two that are worth *using* as well as worth money. Both are
tried first, in a fixed order — potion, then ingredient — and only on a share
that clears `COIN_CEILING`, because below that a share is loose coin rather than
an object. `POTIONS` holds what a potion **does**, so the row names the thing and
the content is the effect: the writing pass sees `Potion: Ghost-speech`.

Existing: `TREASURE_ITEMS`, `VALUABLE_MATERIALS`, `WEAPON_ITEMS`
(`blueprint.ts:353–361`).

**To be filled**, as empty arrays in the same block with `CASCADE_TABLES` keys
wired so `{Misc Item}` and friends resolve like the existing references:

```
MISC_ITEMS  WORN_ITEMS  TOOL_ITEMS  TREASURE_TRAITS  MAGICAL_INGREDIENTS  POTIONS
```

### 3.3 The pot

```
pot = size × rng.range(250, 400)
```

Partitioned across every treasure parcel before anything reaches the LLM, which
is what retires `ParcelGrade`: value stops being a function of grade, and grade
stops existing. Which table a treasure parcel rolls on is decided by the share it
drew, not the other way round.

Level does not move money.

Held back from the partition:

- **10% of the pot**, as the game master's ad-hoc allowance (§7.1).
- **A goal slice**, released or redistributed after the content pass (§6).

`VALUE_BANDS`, `rollItemValue`, and `gradeFor` all go. Three call sites break:
`entities-pass.ts:428`, `content-pass.ts:1518` (the goal item),
and `equipment.test.ts`. The note at `apply.ts:610` about a grade the game master
might name goes with them.

### 3.4 What a key is worth

A key item is **any item placed to answer a challenge** — a vial of acid, a
censer, a coil of chain. "Key" is the role, not the object.

Catalog price where it is a catalog item; a small band well under treasure money
where it is not. Outside the pot either way: the pot is the dungeon's wealth and
a key is equipment. The band stays low so selling a key never beats using it.

## 4. Placement

In order:

**1. General rewards.** Every general parcel is placed. Each reward-guaranteed
location — dead ends, obstacle rooms, rooms holding hostile creatures — rolls
**loot or key**, and the treasure pot is divvied among whatever loot fell out.
This replaces today's hard guarantee, which took every dead end and obstacle room
off the top and would otherwise leave placed keys with nothing.

**2. Goal keys.** The goal's location on the map, plus 1–3 keyable properties
from the goal room. The goal *entity's* own properties cannot be enumerated here
— §6.

**3. Remaining dead-end and obstacle rooms.** Any that came out of step 1 without
a reward gets a key.

**4. Everything else is reserve.**

### 4.1 Rules

- **One key per challenge.** A creature with a weakness and an asset can carry
  two keys, one per property.
- **Goal challenges are the exception**: each gets a placed key *and* stays in
  the reserve to be drawn a second time.
- **No cap per room or per entity.** More than one reward in a room is fine, and
  more than one attached to an entity is fine. Sometimes you stumble on an
  awesome find.
- **A placed key is never behind the thing it opens.** For a blocked-way key:
  remove that edge, BFS from the entrance, and place only in rooms still
  reachable. Reserve keys need no such check — they arrive where the player is.

---

## 5. The reserve

The pool is pure generation data: `(challenge, kind, the mechanical fact)`. **No
prose.** A reserve entry is written only when it is drawn, so its form can match
what the player is doing — information from an NPC mid-conversation, an object in
the cabinet they are searching.

Consequences worth stating:

- The keys pass shrinks to the *placed* set only, roughly four entries at size 5
  rather than thirty.
- A drawn key's fiction is invented under a turn's time pressure, so the draw
  payload hands the game master the target's `base` even when the player has never
  seen the target, plus the rolled fact. Prompt assembly already runs `roomView`
  with `includeHidden: true`, so this is the existing norm rather than a new
  leak.

The game master picks from the offered list; the applier refuses any id not in the
pool, already drawn, or already resolved. The prompt encourages picking something
nearby or thematically tied to what the player is doing. If that produces weird
results, the fallback is trimming the offered list in code — not moving the
choice.

## 6. The keys pass

`src/generation/content/passes/keys-pass.ts`, mirroring the seams quartet
(`buildKeysPassPayload` / `renderKeysPassPrompt` / response schema /
`applyKeysPass`), wired into `src/generation/generate.ts` after the seams pass. One
call per dungeon.

It exists because a placed key and its challenge are usually in different chunks,
and the content pass is per-chunk and parallel — the chunk holding the key cannot
name a door that has not been written yet. This is the same problem the seams
pass was built for, and the same solution.

It does two jobs:

1. **Writes the placed keys' prose**, with both the key and its challenge's
   finished `base` in front of it. Where the challenge is a blocked way, it may
   also rewrite that connector's `conditional` so lock and key name each other.
2. **Mints the goal entity's keys.** The goal's *form* is the content pass's call
   — `GoalDirective` deliberately does not pick the entity type — so its keyable
   properties do not exist until then. The keys pass is the first point at which
   they do, and it writes them in the same call.

The content pass leaves placed keys nameless, the way it already leaves seams
alone.

**The goal slice.** Whether the goal is treasure is likewise the content pass's
call. So the pot is partitioned with a slice held aside, and once the goal's form
is known the slice is either kept by the goal or redistributed proportionally
across the placed treasure. Values are numbers no player has seen yet, so
adjusting them after the fact is safe.

## 7. Runtime

### 7.1 Drawing

A draw mints the key where the fiction puts it — `addChild` for an object, or
plainly told to the player for information — and marks the reserve entry spent.
The 10% allowance is money, spent as coin grants with values drawn by code.

A grant must be justified by the action that earned it: found in the rubble they
were digging through, given by the thing they talked down, carried by the thing
they killed. An item that simply appears in a room they already searched is the
same broken promise as an invented ledge.

### 7.2 Recording the payoff

**The fact a key reveals is a fact about the world, not about the player.** A
journal saying the ghoul is driven back by chimes means the ghoul *is* driven
back by chimes. So it is recorded on the thing itself, at the precision of the
property it answers, and worded as the fact and nothing else:

```
weakness: "Bells"   →   weakness: "Bells and chimes"
```

Exact wording is the whole point — it is what stops the fact drifting between
calls.

It goes in **`state` on the target entity**. No new machinery: `state` is already
writable on every entity kind, and `setState` marking `needUpdate` is the
behaviour we want — the target's description gets rewritten to account for the
newly-known fact the next time the player is in front of it. A connector's
`conditional` is writable on the same terms where the refinement is about what
the blocker actually wants.

That the fact reaches `composed`, and so the player, is not a leak: they are the
ones who just learned it.

**One known limit.** `state` is per-entity, and a rolled property like a
weakness lives on the group's template, so a fact learned about one ghoul is not
attached to the other eleven. In practice the challenge names a specific
creature and that is usually the one that matters. If it bites, the fix is a
fan-out in the resolution handler — write the same `state` to every creature
sharing the `blueprintId` — and that is a small change that does not need
designing now.

For item keys the link also lives in the item's own `state`, so it travels in the
pack and the full PC sheet re-renders it every turn.

`fictionNote` is **not** used for this. `run.fiction` is dungeon-scope, and a
creature's weakness is not a fact about the dungeon.

### 7.3 Maps

A region key reveals rooms: outline, name, connectors, and the non-hidden
fixtures the mapmaker would have noted, drawn from `base` — what the map
recorded, not what is there now.

Mapped rooms are **display only**. `travel.ts` is untouched and `shortestPath`
keeps refusing them: a player does not get to walk through a room they have never
entered.

This needs a `mapped` set in run state rather than any change to what `composed`
means. Fog is `composed === undefined` and that keeps meaning exactly one thing —
never encountered — which is what `mapView` and `needUpdate` both rest on.

## 8. Accomplishments and XP

### 8.1 The ledger

Per challenge, a record of every action aimed at it: rolls and results, damage
taken, turn number. **It never enters the prompt**, so it costs nothing per turn.

Code writes it without the model's help — the turn loop already resolves an
action's target (`steps/1-determine-target.md`), so when that target maps to a
challenge the applier appends the roll outcomes itself.

It must be first-class run state, not the journal: the journal defaults to no
sink and is explicitly "shaped for nobody", so XP cannot read from it.

### 8.2 What resolves a challenge

| challenge | resolved by |
|---|---|
| a creature | killed, persuaded, deceived, driven off, or slipped past — all equal |
| a blocked way | cleared, **or** passed without clearing |
| a hazard | disabled, sprung safely, or circumvented |
| an obstacle | solved or defeated |
| a concealed way | found |
| the objective | **two challenges** — found, then completed |

Entering a room is not an accomplishment.

**Bypass is inferred at tally, not detected in play.** A creature is bested if the
player encountered it and then achieved something afterward — reached or
completed the goal, or resolved another challenge. Two timestamps compared at the
end; no model judgement and no per-turn detection. It correctly refuses to credit
a creature the player fled and never got past.

### 8.3 The tally

One call at end of run, reading the ledger, rating each accomplishment's risk
with the whole history in front of it. Knave's numbers:

```
low 50    moderate 100    high 200        level at 1000
```

Judging risk after the fact is strictly better than judging it in the heat of the
turn, and it takes the XP call off the game master entirely.

**A run that ends in death does not tally.** The ledger is still written, for the
journal and for the record.

**Neither does one the player abandoned** — settled when the end-of-run screen
was built, and not what this section originally said. Abandoning is not an ending
in the fiction: the character is still standing where they were left and nothing
came back up with them, so there is nothing to pay for. `shouldTally` refuses
both, `session.abandon` makes no call at all, and the ledger is kept either way.

**What a survivor keeps, and where the XP goes.** Walking out alive is what makes
the pack yours — `goal_met` and `escaped` both, since whether the objective came
too decides which ending it was and nothing else. `carriedOut` is the one place
that turns a run's character back into the roster's, so the run's award is added
there, in the same transaction as the ending. Death and abandonment write
nothing, which is how they forfeit the gear and the experience together without
either path having to remember to.

Levelling was a separate session and is now built — `src/rules/knave/level-up.ts`,
and `BUILD-PLAN.md` for the decisions. Nothing above it changed: the arithmetic
(~30 challenges in a size 5 dungeon against a 1000-per-level track) stands, and
`PlayerCharacter.xp` is still a career total nothing spends. `level` is moved by
`applyLevelUp` and by nothing else.

## 9. Build order

**R1 — generation, pure, headless.** The challenge enumeration; the new taxonomy;
the pot and its partition; placement in the order at §4; the reserve pool; the
table stubs. Testable entirely under vitest with no LLM: assert that every
dungeon enumerates its challenges, that no placed key sits behind its own lock,
that the treasure placed sums to the pot less its held-back slices, and that the
same seed gives the same pool.

**R2 — the keys pass.** The quartet, the goal-entity keys, the goal slice
resolution, and the content pass learning to skip placed keys.

**R3 — runtime.** The draw; the resolution write; `mapped` and its rendering;
the challenge ledger; the end-of-run tally.

One phase per session, R1 first.

## 10. Left open

- **Placed/reserved ratio** is a dial. §4 fixes the order; the weights inside it
  get tuned once a dungeon's worth of them can be looked at.
- **Whether a dead character's ledger is worth anything at all**, or is only ever
  read by the journal. Partly answered: the end-of-run screen reads the run
  rather than the ledger for what killed them and what they were carrying, so a
  death has something to show without the ledger being scored.
- **The XP arithmetic**, deferred to the levelling session with the numbers from
  §8.3 visible.
